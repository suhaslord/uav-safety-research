# Abstract Draft

Autonomous landing systems can fail when visual perception remains internally consistent while being systematically wrong. This simulation-only study investigates whether confidence-aware redundancy, temporal estimation, calibrated abstention, and explicit evidence provenance can expose such failures without making autonomous landing unusably conservative. AegisLand was developed through a sequence of measured failure modes. In an early 10,000-episode abstract-perception benchmark, a frozen V3 architecture using an independent lower-rate reference estimate reduced mixed-degradation unsafe touchdowns from 84.2% for baseline and 84.0% for the temporal V2 architecture to 2.4%, with 97.6% landing success. Later synthetic-image work culminated in Phase 6B, where component-specific lateral and altitude confidence achieved 99% success and 1% unsafe touchdown under the frozen mixed-degradation benchmark, while preserving an explicit low-light availability cost.

The project then moved toward external validity rather than continuing to optimize the original simulator. Phase 8 compared AegisLand traces with PX4 SITL + Gazebo and preserved a `diagnostic_mismatch` result. Phase 9 archived genuine Gazebo camera payloads and showed that strong target detection did not imply trustworthy metric geometry. Phase 10 (AegisT10) added causal temporal metric state and development-frozen uncertainty calibration. On its frozen Gazebo-camera holdout, point estimates matched the already centimeter-accurate Phase 9 ArUco observations rather than achieving the preregistered substantial error reduction; however, uncertainty honesty improved sharply, with median normalized residuals of 0.646 lateral and 0.521 altitude.

Phase 10R targeted edge and partial-view generalization without retuning the historical Phase 10 holdout. A trajectory-held-out validation showed improved availability and strong in-domain calibration but missed several lateral magnitude gates. The candidate was then frozen at commit `e1d566f8baa47bf10f9bdf39dd5988724208be80` and exposed exactly once to a new protected holdout containing 12 new geometry trajectories, three appearance conditions, 36 sequences, and 1,440 truth-visible frames. The final all-gates result was mixed / failed overall. Ambiguous-view lateral and altitude MAE improved by 79.2% and 73.7%, respectively, and clean-observation MAE did not regress. However, lateral and altitude p95 improvements were only -1.1% and 7.3%, the truth-visible miss rate was 20.0%, and development-frozen 95% uncertainty coverage fell to 84.3% lateral and 79.7% altitude under the harder shift. No false positives were observed.

The frozen Phase 10R result separates three properties that can otherwise be conflated: average point-estimate accuracy, difficult-tail behavior, and calibration transfer under distribution shift. The candidate improved typical ambiguous-view geometry while leaving a hard tail and becoming under-covering outside its earlier calibration domain. This motivates a next phase focused on domain-shift-aware perception reliability and principled abstention rather than post-hoc threshold retuning. All reported evidence is simulation-only; `safety_acceptance = false`, and no physical-aircraft safety or certification claim is made.
