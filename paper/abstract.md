# Abstract Draft

Autonomous landing systems can fail when visual perception remains internally consistent while being systematically wrong. This simulation-only study investigates whether confidence-aware redundancy can expose such failures without making autonomous landing unusably conservative. AegisLand was developed through a sequence of measured failure modes: a static threshold supervisor reduced unsafe touchdowns by aborting excessively; temporal reasoning restored availability but could not reliably identify persistent bias from a single stream; a frozen V3 architecture then introduced an independent lower-rate surrogate reference estimate, persistent disagreement reasoning, and redundant state fusion. In a 10,000-episode held-out abstract-perception benchmark, V3 reduced mixed-degradation unsafe touchdowns from 84.2% for baseline and 84.0% for the temporal V2 architecture to 2.4%, with 97.6% landing success.

The study then replaced direct corrupted-state perception with synthetic pixel sequences. Phase 6B adds temporal image tracking, robust image-derived lateral velocity, separate calibrated lateral and altitude confidence, component-wise abstention, and a simulation-specific pixel-scale observability limit that prevents the altitude confidence model from claiming precision unsupported by the rendered image scale. In a preregistered held-out comparison of 1,500 simulated image-based landing episodes, mixed degradation produced 57% success / 43% unsafe touchdown for image-only temporal perception, 94% / 6% for the established Phase 6 Aegis integration, and 99% / 1% for Phase 6B. Under occlusion, the corresponding results were 86% / 14%, 93% / 7%, and 96% / 4%. Phase 6B preserved 100% success in clean and blur conditions; under low light it achieved 97% success, 0% unsafe touchdown, and 3% timeout. No Phase 6B safe aborts occurred in the held-out landing run.

A separate held-out audit of 10,000 synthetic frames showed strongly selective altitude confidence under blur, low-light, and mixed degradation at the frozen operating point, while lateral confidence under mixed degradation remained a measurable limitation. These results support the narrower hypothesis that component-specific reliability and independent error structure can improve handling of degraded image perception in this controlled simulator. They do not establish real-world UAV safety: the imagery, degradation process, planar vehicle model, pixel-observability rule, and secondary reference sensor are synthetic and require validation with more realistic sensing, dynamics, and correlated-failure models.
