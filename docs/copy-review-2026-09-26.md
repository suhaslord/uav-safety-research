# Site copy review · 26 September 2026

## Verdict

The site explained the same research boundary in too many nearby blocks. The homepage repeated the staleness and Phase 22 story in its hero, finding, researcher cards, method cards, and full verdict timeline. Archive cards repeated a title, identity, summary, central question, and signal. Older phase pages front-loaded a long introduction before readers reached the result. Phase 23 and 24 repeated their headline before the condition table and charts.

## Writing research applied

- [Muñoz-Ortiz, Gómez-Rodríguez, and Vilares (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11422446/) compared human news articles with LLM-generated news and found differences in sentence-length and vocabulary distributions. This is a genre-specific comparison, not a test for whether a particular page was written by AI.
- [Geng and Trotta (2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.657/) studied how language associated with LLM writing changes in academic work. A blacklist of supposedly “AI” words is not a reliable editing rule.

Editorial rule for this site: lead with the exact outcome; use concrete verbs and the numbers that matter; say a caveat once near the claim; move detailed methods behind a clear link or disclosure. Preserve failures, sources, and the boundary between simulation and detector results. This is a readability edit, not a claim of human authorship or an attempt to evade detection.

## Remaining copy policy

New phase pages should use a brief headline, one finding, the key metric and its limit, then optional methods/provenance. If a new section repeats an existing takeaway, link to the earlier one instead of rewriting it.
