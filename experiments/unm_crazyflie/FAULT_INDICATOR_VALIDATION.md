This note records the fault-indicator validation run produced by the PR workflow from a fresh, genuine Webots trajectory.

The controller now treats the unavailable pre-first-step GPS sample as zero measured velocity for the first derivative only. The generated baseline starts with finite velocity values and the Webots log contains no invalid NaN motor-velocity commands.

## Detector definition

- Residual indicator: 0.5 s rolling-median normalized innovation squared (NIS).
- Residual threshold: `1.5913458476`, calibrated as 1.20× the maximum nominal post-settling value.
- Dropout indicator: radial position sigma from the Kalman covariance.
- Dropout threshold: `0.0176166889 m`, calibrated as 1.50× the maximum nominal post-settling value.
- Detector arming: 5 s.
- Persistence: 0.1 s, applied causally. The reported alert time is the sample that confirms the required streak, not the beginning of the streak.
- False-alarm denominators include only armed samples for which the indicator is finite/observable.

The current residual threshold does not detect the 5–10 cm fixed-bias cases. It does detect the 20–40 cm cases, but with modest recall under the persistent alert definition. Dropout detection is driven by covariance growth because no residual exists while measurements are absent.

## Trial-count rule

Gaussian-noise faults are stochastic and use 30 independently seeded trials per severity. The bias and dropout injections are deterministic in this experiment, so each severity is evaluated once rather than repeating an identical run under different seed labels.

## Interpretation boundary

These are simulation-only results. The trajectory itself is generated by Webots, but the measurement faults are injected offline into the saved trajectory for controlled comparison. The thresholds are calibrated from the same nominal trajectory used for this evaluation, so this is an in-sample validation result, not evidence of generalization to different trajectories, vehicles, environments, or physical flight.

The strongest next validation step is a separate no-fault/holdout Webots trajectory with changed motion timings and speeds while keeping the detector definitions and thresholds frozen.
