Research question

When the landing estimate gets worse, can the system tell that it is getting worse?

The project separates three things that are easy to mix together: how wrong the estimate is, how wide the uncertainty interval is, and how predictable the next small error step looks. The point is to see when those signals agree — and when they do not.

UncertaintyDoes the reported interval still cover the actual error under degradation?
Absolute errorHow much does staleness change lateral accuracy and coverage?
Short-horizon behaviorCan local residual structure stay predictable even while the overall estimate gets worse?
Key caution

A stale estimate can be less accurate while an old uncertainty width barely changes. Later residual structure does not mean latency improved the underlying level error.

Main conclusion

Confidence, absolute error, and local predictability are not the same thing.

Across the frozen simulation studies, a stale estimate can become less accurate while its old uncertainty width barely changes. At the same time, short-horizon residual behavior can still have repeatable structure. Later phases found that much of the synthetic context effect could be described with simple first-order terms and transferred to fresh 32-cell context cubes without refitting.

Main purpose

Keep the full record, including the parts that did not work.

Each phase answers one narrower question. Candidates are frozen before later evidence is opened, failed gates stay failed, and a later success does not rewrite an earlier miss. That makes the archive useful as a research record instead of a highlight reel.

Method

Freeze the decision before opening the next result.

Thresholds, coefficients, and context definitions are locked before protected evidence is exposed. If a gate fails, the phase closes with that failure. The next phase can ask a different question, but it cannot go back and tune the old answer.
