AegisLand follows that problem through simulated UAV landing experiments, including the misses. The scientific simulation record is frozen at Phase 22. A separate Phase 24 audit examines published detector results on real-camera data.

Main conclusion · The result that shaped the later work

Error grew before the old uncertainty interval reacted enough.

That gap led to a second question: even when the level error gets worse, is there still structure in how the residual changes from one step to the next? Phases 19–22 follow that narrower question.

What the project does · The measurement problem

Find when landing perception becomes confidently wrong.

Research question: Does the uncertainty match the error? We compare three things: the actual error, the reported uncertainty interval, and how the residual changes from one step to the next.

Why the archive keeps the misses

A failed gate stays failed, even when the next experiment works.

Each phase is judged under the rule it had when its evidence was opened. Later phases can ask a better question, but they cannot rewrite an earlier verdict.

How a verdict is protected

Set the rule before opening the evidence that will judge it.

Candidates, thresholds, and coefficients are frozen before protected evaluation. If a locked check misses, the phase records the miss instead of moving the target.

Freeze what will be judged.

The candidate, thresholds, and scientific code are fixed before protected data are opened.

01 · Freeze

Do not recycle the same evidence.

Development data cannot later be counted as protected validation or as the final holdout.

02 · Separate

Record the miss as a miss.

A failed phase can motivate the next experiment, but its own verdict does not change.

03 · Preserve
