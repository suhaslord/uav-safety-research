Research question

When synthetic UAV landing perception becomes stale or degraded, can frozen uncertainty and local error-dynamics diagnostics distinguish confidence from correctness, explain which contexts drive failure, and transfer those patterns without refitting?

Main purpose

Build an inspectable research record, not a demo that only shows wins.

AegisLand separates uncertainty calibration, absolute estimation quality, failure attribution, and local one-step predictability. Candidates are frozen before downstream evidence, negative results stay public, and later questions are narrowed rather than rewriting earlier failures.

Problem being studied

A system can look confident while its estimate is wrong.

Under synthetic degradation, interval width, actual level error, and short-horizon residual dynamics do not have to move together. The research asks which of those quantities remain informative—and where a proposed recoverability or predictive claim breaks.

Approach

Freeze first. Expose evidence once.

The protocol is designed so a protected or final result cannot be tuned into a pass after it is seen. Later phases may ask a narrower new question, but they do not retroactively change an earlier verdict.

01 / FREEZE

Lock the candidate.

Coefficients, thresholds, context definitions, and scientific code are frozen before downstream evidence is exposed.

02 / SEPARATE

Use fresh evidence roles.

Development, transfer, protected validation, and final holdout are treated as sequential evidence rather than one reusable test set.

03 / MATCH

Control the intervention.

Matched synthetic comparisons isolate pure two-frame staleness while preserving truth, pair identity, useful masks, and Phase 12 interval widths.

04 / PRESERVE

Keep failed gates failed.

Phase 14, Phase 15, Phase 17, and Phase 18 do not disappear because later, narrower questions eventually passed.

Main conclusion

Uncertainty, level error, and local predictability are different things.

Across the frozen simulation studies, pure two-frame stale estimates can worsen absolute lateral error while leaving Phase 12 interval widths unchanged. A context-specific one-step residual structure can still be predictable, and later phases show that much of its synthetic context dependence is first-order and can be approximated additively on fresh 32-cell context cubes. This does not mean latency is beneficial.
