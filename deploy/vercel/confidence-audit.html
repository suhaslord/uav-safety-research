Phase 25 · read-only diagnostic · 11 October 2026

A high score
isn't always right.

AegisLand's detector can produce a correct landing-pad box and still assign its highest confidence to a different, incorrect box. That difference matters before any controller relies on the selected prediction.

01 / A different question

What gets trusted?

The earlier any-match score asks whether some detection matched every labeled target. A downstream system might instead choose the single highest-confidence box. This read-only analysis selects that top box without looking at the ground truth, then checks whether it actually overlaps a labeled landing pad at IoU ≥ 0.50.

A frame can have a correct candidate somewhere in its detections while the top candidate is incorrect. Across six reused conditions and both models, 135 dependent model/view instances had this disagreement (88 baseline and 47 Phase 23). These instances are not independent flight events.

02 / Concrete failure examples

High confidence cannot be assumed safe.

Even a high detector score is not a calibrated probability of safe landing. Illustrative retrospective score cutoffs show how false confidence and near-total abstention can both occur. These cutoffs are not deployment settings.

Score cutoff 0.50

Out of 86 dependent occlusion views, an illustrative 0.50 cutoff kept two top detections. Both top boxes were wrong. A higher score alone would not have rescued that example.

Phase 23 / mixed stress0 / 86

Score cutoff 0.25

The illustrative 0.25 cutoff accepted no mixed-stress views. A rule that abstains on everything can avoid accepting bad boxes while failing to supply any usable coverage.

No thresholds were tuned.

These cutoff calculations describe previously used protected data. They cannot select a Phase 26 accept/reject threshold; tuning requires genuinely new development data, followed by sealed independent evaluation.

03 / Scientific boundary

Useful diagnosis.
Not a deployment verdict.

The frozen dataset contains 86 KIOS frames, two original video sessions, and six conditions reconstructed from each frame. The resulting 1,032 paired model/view rows are dependent. Every frame has a labeled landing target. There are no independent empty-pad negative scenes, no new test sessions, and no tested confidence-to-safety calibration.

What was verified

The four frozen Phase 25 input tables were SHA-256 verified, the original 86 × 6 × 2 inventory reconciled, and the read-only top-candidate analysis archived without retraining or modifying the source evidence.

What remains unverified

False acceptance in no-target images, safe-to-proceed decisions, new camera-domain generalization, integrated Model VASE controller response, physical flight performance, and Phase 26 independent dataset admission.

Phase 26 status: NO_DATASET_ADMITTED.

The next admissible test needs independent real-camera sessions, negative frames, owner permissions, matching pad annotations and duplicate screening against KIOS; select a rule on development data before testing sealed sessions once. H1/H2 motor-fault simulations do not answer this landing-perception question.

04 / Evidence trail

Everything traceable.

Original frozen tables are unchanged. The Actions artifact preserves all 1,032 top-selection decisions, a summary, and the source run identity. The artifact has finite retention and should be preserved independently for external review.
